Our Story

Exeter, pasta, and looking after people

Berua has been building businesses on Longbrook Street for years. The barber shop first, then the cocktail bar, and now Pasta 29. Same street, same people, same reason.

There are three of us behind it. A trip to Italy started the pasta, Mauro brought twenty years of kitchens, and all three of us love the hospitality side of it: the welcome, the regulars, the looking after people.

That is the whole story. We love this city, we love pasta, and we love feeding people properly.

Dasaw

A Kurdish word. The customer comes first, and then everything you put into the food after that: how carefully you prepare it, how you season it, how it tastes. All of it for the person eating it.

Customer first

Good food with poor service brings nobody back. Looking after people comes before anything else.

Quality first

Good cheese, fresh pasta in every day, sauces cooked slow. We would rather break even for six months than serve something we are not proud of.

Made for the street

Grab and go, or sit in with a coffee. Built for a street full of students and regulars.

Head Chef

Mauro

Twenty years in Exeter kitchens

He opened Zizzi on Gandy Street in 2003 as its first head chef, stayed eight years, then ran a kitchen in Princesshay for seven more. Ask him about the lasagne and he will tell you it takes two days: ragù cooked low and rested overnight, then layered the next morning with béchamel, parmesan and basil.
