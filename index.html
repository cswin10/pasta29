Our Story

For the love of pasta

It started with a trip to Italy. We went, we ate, we fell for it completely — the food, the people, the way a good bowl of pasta quietens a table for a second. We came home wanting to cook it for Exeter.

That's the whole reason. Not a business plan. Pasta, made properly, for people we know.

Why Longbrook Street

Because it's ours. We opened a barber shop on this street. We opened a cocktail bar on this street. We know the faces — the students, the regulars, the people who walk past at eight in the morning and again at eleven at night.

This street has been good to us. We wanted to give it something it didn't have: somewhere to sit down, take a breath, have a coffee and a proper plate of pasta, and know the people who made it. A European feel, in your own neighbourhood.

A family business

Three of us, one kitchen. You'll meet us — we're the ones behind the counter. That's rather the point.

Dasaw

A Kurdish word. It means the customer comes first — and then everything you put into the food after that: how carefully you prepare it, how you season it, how it tastes. All of it, for the person eating it.

It's the whole business in one word.

Customer first

If the food is good and the service isn't, nobody comes back. We look after people. That comes before everything.

Quality over margin

Good cheese. Fresh pasta in every day. Sauces cooked slow, never hurried. We'd rather break even for six months than serve something we aren't proud of — people travel for food worth travelling for.

Made for the neighbourhood

Easy food, healthy food, priced for a street full of students and regulars. Grab and go, or stay a while.

Head Chef

Mauro

Twenty years in Exeter kitchens

Mauro has been cooking in this city since 2003, when he opened a restaurant on Gandy Street as its first head chef. Eight years there, then seven more running a kitchen in Princesshay. He has cooked for a thousand people a week.

Ask him about the lasagne and he'll tell you it takes two days. The ragù goes on low for two hours and rests overnight. The next morning it's layered with béchamel, parmesan and basil, three layers deep, and baked. Twenty portions at a time. No shortcuts, because the shortcuts are the bit you can taste.

Good to know

Vegetarian and vegan options on the menu. Any dish can be made gluten-free — we just change the pasta. Please tell us about allergies when you order.

Alongside the six, we'll run a Pasta of the Week — one dish, rotating, in the shop and on our socials. Worth checking before you come.
