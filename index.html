Our Story

A Long Way
from Italy

Berua grew up in Kyrgyzstan, a long way from the olive groves and pasta kitchens of Italy. But food has a way of travelling. A jar of proper San Marzano tomatoes, a plate of handmade tagliatelle at a friend's table, a trip to Bologna that changed everything — these small encounters became a quiet obsession. Not with restaurants or fine dining, but with the everyday Italian way of caring about ingredients.

He spent years learning — from nonnas who would only share recipes after the third visit, from producers who spoke about their olive oil the way poets speak about light. He learned that Italian food isn't about complexity. It's about respect: for the grain, the tomato, the slow afternoon.

Pasta 29 is the result of all of that. A small delicatessen on Longbrooke Street where the pasta is made fresh, the antipasti are prepared by hand, and the pantry shelves hold things worth crossing town for. Nothing rushed. Nothing fake. Just honest food, made with care.

“Italian food taught me that the best things are simple — you just have to care enough to do them properly.”
